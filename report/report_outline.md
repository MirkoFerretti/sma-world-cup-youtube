# Report Outline

## Objective

- Introduce the project topic and why World Cup YouTube discussions are relevant for Social Media Analytics.
- State the research questions clearly.
- Explain why the project focuses on narrative communities rather than only sentiment.

## Data

- Describe the selected YouTube videos and why they were chosen.
- Explain what metadata and comment fields were collected.
- Report the time period, language considerations, and any sampling constraints.
- Document cleaning steps and final dataset scope.

## Models

- Explain the user-video bipartite network and the user-user co-commenting projection.
- Define Network A and Network B and justify both thresholds.
- Describe Louvain community detection and network metrics.
- Describe sentiment, emotion, TF-IDF, and entity extraction methods with cautions.

## Results

- Present community structure and key network findings.
- Compare the dominant narratives across communities.
- Identify bridge users, videos, teams, players, or controversy topics.
- Interpret results carefully without overclaiming what the methods prove.

## Visualization

- Include readable network figures and community size summaries.
- Show supporting tables or charts for keywords, entities, sentiment, and emotions.
- Explain what each visualization adds to the argument.

## Limitations

- Discuss the limits of co-commenting as a proxy for shared discussion.
- Note the limitations of Louvain, lexicon methods, NER, and multilingual or sarcastic comments.
- Mention sampling bias from selected videos and API availability.

## Conclusion

- Summarize the main contribution of the project.
- Revisit the research questions with evidence-based answers.
- Suggest realistic next steps or extensions.
